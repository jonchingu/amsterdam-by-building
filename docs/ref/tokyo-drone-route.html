Route anchors

The drone passes through every anchor in order on a smooth curve. At each one it holds the anchor's framing and zoom; between two anchors it eases from one shot to the next. A hold above zero stops the drone there; zero flies straight through. Aim an anchor at a building and the camera keeps that building in frame, and two anchors aimed at the same spot make a tracking shot. Your route is saved in this browser.     How the camera is driven

    PathA centripetal Catmull–Rom curve through the anchor positions, so corners round off without overshooting into walls. Legs that would pass through a building are flagged in red.
    SpeedEach leg has a cruise speed. The drone eases out of a stop and into the next, and keeps its speed through anchors with no hold, so there is no lurch at each anchor.
    Framing and zoomHeading, tilt and zoom follow their own smooth curves from anchor to anchor. Zoom changes the lens (field of view), not the drone's position, and blends evenly on a log scale.
    Taking overAs with MapKit's keyframe animator, any input during playback stops the animation and leaves you flying from that exact spot. Play again blends back onto the route.
    SharingA tour link carries the route itself, compressed into the part of the link after #, so nothing is uploaded. Record captures the canvas in real time while the route plays from the start, without the route line, map or readouts.
    Buildings: Tokyo Metropolitan Government, 都市の3Dデジタルマップ 2025 (CC BY 4.0), the same 11 textured LOD3 tiles as the Toranomon survey page. Walls come from a 2 m height grid built from the tiles as they load. It treats buildings as solid from the roof down, so the drone can't fly under overhangs.
